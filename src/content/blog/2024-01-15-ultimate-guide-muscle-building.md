The original article covered muscle building. This revised topic focuses on the planning questions around a training routine, without presenting universal exercise prescriptions or promising a particular result.

## Define what matters to you

Describe your reason for wanting to train in your own words. Consider how it relates to your work, daily responsibilities and the activities you enjoy.

You do not need to borrow somebody else’s target. A clear personal goal gives you something useful to discuss when deciding what support or instruction you need.

## Build around your actual schedule

Look for times you could reasonably protect during a normal week. Consider travel, caring responsibilities, access to equipment and the time needed to get to and from a session.

Questions to bring to a conversation include:

- Which parts of the week are predictable?
- What tends to disrupt your plans?
- What would a manageable starting routine look like?
- Who could help you make that time available?

## Keep a useful record

A simple note of what you planned, what happened and what got in the way can support a coaching discussion. It can help you distinguish an unrealistic schedule from an adjustment you want to make.

This page does not provide an individual exercise program. For technique instruction or a program suited to your circumstances, seek appropriate professional support. Discuss pain, injury, health conditions or exercise restrictions with a qualified clinician.
